Common Excel Spreadsheet Issues

When it comes to Excel, businesses can run into all sorts of problems. From incorrect formulas to inconsistency in data, a lot can go wrong. In order to minimise these issues, it’s crucial for businesses to be aware of the most common spreadsheet problems and how to avoid them.

Inconsistent Data

One of the most common spreadsheet problems is inconsistency in data. This can happen when different people are inputting data into different parts of the sheet or when cells are copied and pasted without being updated.

To avoid this issue, it’s important to have a clear data entry system and always double-check your work.

Incorrect Formulas

Another common problem is incorrect formulas. This can happen when people try to do too much in a single cell or copy and paste formulas without checking them first.

We find it important to use clear and concise formulas and always check your work before submitting it.

Hidden Data

Sometimes data can be hidden in cells without people realising it. This can happen when cells are formatted as text or when someone uses a filter to hide certain data.

Make sure your business is aware of all the formatting options available in Excel, and always check through your filters – this could be the issue!

These are just a few of the most common spreadsheet problems businesses can run into. By being aware of these issues and taking steps to avoid them, you can help ensure that your Excel files are as accurate and consistent as possible.

Types Of Excel Group Training

There are different types of Excel Group training, and they vary depending on the skill level your employees already have. You don’t want to waste time teaching them what they already know, but it is vital to understand the basic functions.

Excel For Beginners

Excel for beginners training would start with the absolute basics, essentially assuming that the students have never used this software before. It can also help to get rid of bad and time-consuming habits they may have picked up.

Training for beginners would teach the students how to use the basic functions of a spreadsheet and how to put one together. This will include drag and dropping, how to use the fill function, and how to format cells. 

Intermediate Excel Training

Intermediate Excel training will assume that the employees have a handle on the absolute basics of Excel and introduce them to some of the more complex functions of the software. 

These functions include creating graphs and charts from data, using functions to filter information on a spreadsheet, and the functions that will allow them to find and sort data quickly and effectively. 

Advanced Excel Group Courses

If your employees are already confident with the basic and intermediate functions of Excel, then an advanced course is a great choice. This will take them beyond the day-to-day functions and show them just what the software has to offer.

This course will teach them how to use Excel functions to create data simulations to predict patterns and project budgets. They will learn how to create their own Macros, which are specific command tools, and pivot tables.

Pivot Tables

Creating tables is one of the most valuable skills that anyone learning about Excel can have, and it is vital for anyone who works in finance or statistics. 

A pivot table is essentially a way to sort, organise, count data. If you have a huge amount of data, a pivot table allows you to either analyse specific elements or to neatly summarise what you are dealing with. It is invaluable for presentations, for example.

Power Excel Group Courses

One of the reasons why Excel is so important to businesses is that it has the ability to perform advanced mathematical equations with a simple formula. The POWER function is one of the most commonly used.

Say that the number you are working with is the base. The power is the number to which the base number is raised to. For example, to get a result that is 25, the POWER function would be (5,2), which is five squared.

What is Excel Recruitment?

As mentioned above, excel recruitment is simply the act of using Microsoft Excel in order to track the progress of the applicants or respondents that you have had apply for a vacancy within your business. 

You can easily build a spreadsheet that houses all of the pertinent information such as their contact info, education level, whether they have experience or not, interview scheduling and results. You can update the spreadsheet throughout the process to track the progression of the applicants. 

The Benefits of Excel Recruitment

Utilising Microsoft Excel throughout your recruitment process has a number of benefits. First and foremost,  it really does have the potential to make the process easier and more streamlined.  

  • Keeps all of the necessary information in one place.
  • Makes it easier to track the progression of the applicants.
  • Allows you to rank applicants, making it easier to work out your first, second and third choice.

In short, it makes it easier to measure the applicants against each other. If you then found that your first choice was unavailable, you could consult the spreadsheet and reach out to your second choice without having to go back through reams of paperwork and information.

How to Use Excel for Recruitment Purposes

Using Microsoft Excel for recruitment purposes might sound a little daunting or confusing, but it couldn’t be simpler. You don’t necessarily have to know all of the spreadsheet codes either; with a bit of time and help from the right people and resources, and you can easily construct a recruitment spreadsheet.

For example, the Excel Experts offer solutions to large or small businesses in order to automate the recruitment process or offer solutions to help.

Applications & Notes

First things first. Think about your person specification for the role. Use thins to create headings like ‘experience’, ‘necessary qualifications’ or ‘desired skills’. Then as you go through the applications, you can input the names of the applicants onto the spreadsheet and whether or not they have the attributes that you want. 

Applicant Tracking

Depending on your recruitment process, you might have different requirements like an online questionnaire, a brief test or portfolio submission. An excel spreadsheet allows you to keep track of what applicants have done what and what the results were like, whether they passed the test or not. 

Storing Candidate Information

Instead of having to go through each application individually to find the person you were looking for, you can keep everyone’s contact information in one place. This makes it a lot easier and a lot quicker when it comes to looking up a candidate and their info.

Interview Processes

Every business has its own approach to the interview process. For some businesses, it’s a formality, an opportunity to get a feel for the person behind the CV. For others, it is streamlining process designed to weed out unsuitable candidates.

Either way, you can use an Excel spreadsheet to keep track of the interview schedule and whether the interview went well or not. 

Excel Recruitment Tips and Tricks

Luckily, there are several tips and tricks that you can embrace to ensure that you are making the most out of your Microsoft Excel efforts, especially when it comes to your recruitment processes. 

  • Consult the internet or a business like the Excel Expert for help in constructing the spreadsheet.
  • House the spreadsheet in a shared space to allow for collaboration from other members of the team
  • Ensure that it is comprehensive, with all the relevant information
  • Update the spreadsheet regularly
  • Consider using a template to create the spreadsheet

Advanced Excel Recruitment Functions

Depending on your skill level, whether or not you have a template or have outsourced the production of your spreadsheet, you might find that there are several advanced functions for you to explore. The advanced features can make it even easier to find the frontrunner or best candidate for the role.

Comparing Candidates

Microsoft Excel has a number of different functions. For example, in addition to the mathematical equations that you can do and use in Microsoft Excel, you can also compare data.

This capability then makes it easy to compare candidates; you can compare them in each of your desired specifications to continually weed out unsuitable candidates until you are left with the strongest pool of applicants. You can then use this to decide who to offer an interview to. 

Managing Responses & Submissions

An excel spreadsheet can also be used to determine the replies to the ad, how they found the job ad and whether they have fulfilled all of the submission requirements, including cover letters, assessments, portfolios and questionnaires.

If your applicants cannot fulfil said requirements or they fail them, then you can make a note of this on the spreadsheet and terminate your efforts with that candidate. 

Conduct Screening Tests

As mentioned above, you can use your Excel recruitment process to monitor the results of screening tests. For example, you can keep track of who passed what element.

You can also look for trends; for example, if more people are passing the initial screening test than the interview, it could be a sign that the initial screening test needs to be re-evaluated.
